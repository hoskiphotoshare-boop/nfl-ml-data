How the model works

Games. Logistic regression on pre-game rolling EPA, rest days, team scoring efficiency, Next Gen Stats, snap share and depth-chart role. Trained on nflverse play-by-play from 1999 to today and validated with a walk-forward backtest (pooled AUC ≈ 0.69, log loss ≈ 0.63).

Players. Per-position models project targets, receptions, yards, touchdowns and fantasy points, adjusted for injuries and depth-chart rank. Recent form shows the last six games.

Playoffs. A Monte Carlo simulation plays out the remaining schedule and bracket 10,000 times. Game probabilities blend the match model with an opponent-adjusted point-differential rating, and each simulated season carries team-strength uncertainty for injuries, form and coaching. It counts playoff berths, conference titles and Super Bowl wins.

Model edges. Every priced game gets a model edge for moneyline, spread and total, expressed in probability points against vig-free market prices. The “Model edges” view ranks them; large gaps usually mean the model disagrees strongly with the market, not that a bet is guaranteed.

Futures. Super Bowl winner prices from sportsbooks are normalized to remove the bookmaker overround, then compared with the simulated championship probability on the Playoffs tab.

Freshness. Data refreshes daily. Game odds update twice on weekdays and five times on Sundays around kickoff windows; futures refresh once a day.
